# Iron Forgiveness: Measurable Design and Engineering Data (MOI, CG, Offset, Sole, Construction) and the Highest-MOI Irons

Research notes. Today: 2026-10-14. Method caveat: WebFetch could not resolve several domains (golfdigest.com, mygolfspy.com, todays-golfer.com), so the findings below come from search-engine extracts of those pages, not full-page reads. Figures are labelled **[MFR-CLAIM]** when they come from the manufacturer or are repeated from manufacturer spec sheets, **[INDEPENDENT]** when they come from independent testing (robot or player panel), and **[UNVERIFIED]** when the original source could not be confirmed.

## 1. Which irons have published or measured MOI figures? (values by model)

### Takeaway
Very few independent, same-protocol MOI measurements of irons exist in public. Most published iron MOI numbers are manufacturer figures for a 7-iron, heel-toe axis, through the CG. Super game-improvement (SGI) 7-irons cluster at about 2,900–3,400 g·cm², against about 2,400 for a Ping players' iron and about 2,700 for a "typical" game-improvement (GI) iron. On published figures, the Ping G425 (3,360) and an unverified G710 figure (>3,300) are the highest found. Ping's newer G730 is described as higher than the G430 and G710, but I found no number for it.

### Inferences
- The Ping G-series has the densest published MOI trail (K15 → GMax → G400 → G410 [+8%] → G425 [3,360] → G430 [3,196] → G440; and G700 → G710 [+5%, ~3,300 unverified] → G730 [> G710 and G430]). The G425 holds the highest Ping figure actually published; the G730 is probably at or above it.
- Ping deliberately lowered MOI from G425 to G430 while claiming tighter dispersion. That supports the Golf Digest view that in the SGI class, MOI is close to its useful ceiling and other variables (spin, CG height, face design) now drive results.
- Comparing across brands is unsafe. Manufacturers measure about different axes and points, and with or without the hosel, and none publishes a protocol.

### Gaps

- No independent lab (MyGolfSpy, Golf Digest, Wishon) same-protocol MOI comparison table was found. Wishon's site discusses MOI rankings within his own line only (e.g. 565MC "3rd among all TWGT forged irons for MOI") [MFR-CLAIM]. — [Wishon iron designs](https://wishongolf.com/technology/design-firsts/iron-designs/)
- No vertical (top-bottom) MOI figures were found for any iron.

## 2. How do MOI, CG, offset and sole width relate to accuracy (dispersion, face angle on heel/toe strikes, gear effect)?

### Takeaway
Engineering sources agree that heel-toe MOI mainly limits face twist and ball-speed loss on heel and toe strikes. Iron gear effect is small because the CG sits close to the face. Offset's effect on face angle is a fraction of a degree. Sole width and CG height mainly affect launch and turf interaction, not left-right dispersion. Ping's own data and robot tests show spin and launch consistency now drive dispersion as much as MOI does.

### Cited Findings
- **MOI → ball speed retention:** Ping's Brad Schweigert said the higher-MOI K15 (3,000) keeps about 2–3 mph more ball speed on off-centre strikes than the S56 (2,400), roughly 5 yards [MFR-CLAIM, engineer quote]. — [Golf Digest](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/forgiving-irons)
- **MOI vs dispersion trade-off:** Ping cut G430 MOI to 3,196 from 3,360 on the G425, yet claims about 23% tighter dispersion because the G430 spins less [MFR-CLAIM]. — [Today's Golfer](https://www.todays-golfer.com/news-and-events/equipment-news/2023/ping-g430-irons-promise-big-distance-gains-over-g425)
- **Gear effect in irons is weak:** "Because the center of gravity is typically located much closer to the club face in irons and wedges vs. drivers and metal woods, gear effect is much less pronounced in off-center iron and wedge shots." On a toe hit (right-hander) the face opens and gear effect adds right-to-left (draw) spin. — [Titleist Learning Lab](https://www.titleist.com/learning-lab/performance/golf-gear-effect)
- A hobbyist engineering analysis on GolfWRX applies Tutelman's gear-effect equation (spin ∝ ball speed, CG-to-face depth, impact offset, head MOI). It notes OEM MOI is quoted about a vertical axis through the CG, which matters more at low lofts (drivers) than for irons [not peer-reviewed]. — [GolfWRX "Gear Effect in Irons?"](https://forums.golfwrx.com/topic/1916849-gear-effect-in-irons-diagrams-and-calcs-using-tutelman-cad-model-cgmoi-mpf-data-implications-hybrids-flyers/)
- **CG placement:** Wishon's 1998 TG irons moved the CG 2–3 mm toward the heel to help golfers square the face. Before variable-thickness faces, CG position in cast irons could only be set by blade height or sole width [MFR/designer claim]. — [Wishon iron designs](https://wishongolf.com/technology/design-firsts/iron-designs/)
- **Sole width and CG:** Hireko's fitting guide argues sole width "does not shift the center of gravity back as much as advertising suggests". — [Hireko clubhead geometry guide](https://www.hirekogolf.com/modern-guide-to-golf-club-fitting-clubhead-geometry-in-pictures-irons-wedges)
- **Offset → face angle:** By delaying impact by 3.5 mm, the face travels longer in the arc and arrives "a fraction of a degree more closed (nearly a 0.25°)". Hireko adds that "there are too many other parameters that have changed to honestly access what effect offset itself has on ball flight", because high-offset irons also tend to have longer blades, more upright lies, wider soles and shorter hosels. — [Hireko iron fitting guide](https://www.hirekogolf.com/modern-guide-to-golf-club-fitting-iron-fitting)
- **Offset and launch:** A blog claims offset puts the CG further back for higher launch [low-quality source]. A secondhand claim that 4 of 5 Plugged In Golf testers drew the ball more with more offset could not be verified. — [Golf Influence](https://www.golfinfluence.com/skill/what-is-offset-in-golf-irons/); [GOLF.com zero-offset mailbag](https://golf.com/gear/irons/zero-offset-irons-fully-equipped-mailbag/)
- **Wishon on undercut cavities:** The 989CLA uses a "deep undercut back cavity to increase MOI and off-center hit performance" with only 2 mm offset, showing high MOI can come without much offset [MFR-CLAIM]. — [Wishon 989CLA](https://wishongolf.com/989cla-irons/)
- **Hollow-body and multi-material construction (manufacturer explanations):**
  - G730: bigger head and wider sole raise MOI "in both axes". — [Colorado AvidGolfer](https://coloradoavidgolfer.com/no-stopping-ping/)
  - Callaway's 1997 Tungsten Titanium iron: a tungsten sole insert was "15 percent of the club head volume but 40 percent of the weight".
  - Callaway's 2004 Fusion irons: titanium plus stainless construction raised MOI. — [Golflink Callaway irons by year](https://www.golflink.com/equipment/callaway-irons-by-year-2989678)

### Inferences
- In order of evidence strength for accuracy on mishits:
  1. Heel-toe MOI, which limits face rotation and speed loss on heel and toe strikes.
  2. Low and deep CG, which helps launch and spin consistency on thin and low-face strikes, the most common amateur miss.
  3. Offset, about 0.25° of closure per 3.5 mm. That is real but small, and the evidence is confounded.
  4. Sole width, which mainly affects turf interaction and fat shots, not left-right dispersion.
- Gear effect gives some draw spin on toe strikes that partly cancels face opening. Because the iron CG is shallow, this effect is weak, so heel-toe MOI remains the main lever.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed sports-engineering study isolating iron MOI, offset or sole width against dispersion was found.
- No controlled offset-only test (same head, varied offset) was found.
- No measured CG depth or height figures (mm) were found for any iron models.

## 3. Which irons are regarded by data as the most forgiving ever made, and is there data comparing generations?

### Takeaway
No all-time, same-protocol dataset exists. The best evidence is:
- the published Ping MOI series, where the G425 and G710 are highest and the G730 is claimed higher still;
- Cleveland's Launcher XL and XL Halo figures (about 2,900–3,100);
- recent independent robot and player-panel tests (2023–2026).

The recent tests repeatedly put the following at or near the top for mishit performance: Ping G730, Cleveland ZipCore XL, Callaway Quantum Max OS (tightest dispersion), Cobra King Max (MyGolfSpy forgiveness #1) and Tour Edge Hot Launch Max D (MyGolfSpy accuracy #1).

### Cited Findings
**Generational data (Ping)**
- K15 3,000 → G425 3,360 → G430 3,196 [MFR-CLAIM]; G410 +8% over G400; G710 +5% over G700; G730 > G710 and > G430. — [Golf Digest](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/forgiving-irons); [Today's Golfer](https://www.todays-golfer.com/news-and-events/equipment-news/2023/ping-g430-irons-promise-big-distance-gains-over-g425); [Golfalot G410](https://golfalot.com/equipment-review/ping-g410-irons-review); [Plugged In Golf G710](https://pluggedingolf.com/ping-g710-irons-review/); [MyGolfSpy G730](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/first-look/ping-g730-irons-review/)
- Cleveland Launcher XL Halo is +17% MOI over the predecessor HB Turbo [MFR-CLAIM]. — [MyGolfSpy](https://mygolfspy.com/launcher-xl-halo-irons-clevelands-big-easy/)

**Independent robot tests: GOLF.com ClubTest (Golf Laboratories)**
- 2023 iron test at 95 mph: carry delta of centre vs heel and toe. Smallest toe-miss losses were PXG 0311 XP Gen6 (−6.4 yd) and TaylorMade P770 (−6.8 yd). Mizuno JPX 923 Tour lost only 0.8 yd on low heel. Heel deltas were "noticeably tighter than the toe." Only the G730 and Cleveland ZipCore XL produced single-digit carry deltas [INDEPENDENT]. — [GOLF.com 2024 toe misses](https://golf.com/gear/irons/2024-irons-toe-misses-robot-testing/); [GOLF.com heel misses 2024](https://golf.com/gear/irons/heel-miss-2024-irons-robot-testing/); [GOLF.com consistent contact 2024](https://golf.com/gear/irons/consistent-contact-2024-irons-robot-testing/)

Cobra King Max was widest in the SGI group at 172.7 sq ft, but still tighter than 19 of the 30 irons. **Other**
- Today's Golfer found GI irons the least consistent of four categories, with the widest left-right dispersion averaging 7.55 yd. — [Today's Golfer iron categories 2026](https://www.todays-golfer.com/news-and-events/equipment-news/golf-iron-categories-comparison-2026/)

### Inferences
- If "most forgiving ever" is defined by the highest published heel-toe MOI, the leaders found are the Ping G425 (3,360), the G710 (~3,300+, unverified) and the G730 (claimed higher, no figure), followed by the Cleveland Launcher XL (3,081). If it is defined by measured mishit carry retention, the leaders are the Ping G730 and Cleveland ZipCore XL (GOLF.com 2024). If it is defined by measured dispersion, the leader is the Callaway Quantum Max OS (Golf Digest 2026).
- Classic 2000s SGI irons (Callaway X-18/X-20/Big Bertha, TaylorMade Burner, Cobra, Wilson, Mizuno JPX EZ) have no public MOI or robot data. Any "most forgiving ever" claim for them is anecdotal.
- Robot data show category labels overlap heavily: players' irons like the Mizuno Pro S-1 can beat some GI irons on toe strikes. Real-world forgiveness therefore depends on the specific model, not the category.

### Gaps
- No data comparing all generations (e.g. G Max vs G400 Max vs G425 vs G430 vs G730 in one test) was found.
- Exact heel and toe carry and ball-speed numbers by model for the 2026 Golf Digest SGI test were not retrieved, because full pages could not be fetched.
- No MOI data was found for Wilson Launch Pad, Tour Edge Hot Launch, Mizuno JPX EZ/Hot Metal HL, Cobra F-Max/T-Rail, TaylorMade Burner/RocketBladez Max/Stealth HD/Qi HL, or PXG 0311 XP.

## 4. Where does the Srixon ZX4 (2021) sit on these metrics, and is it GI or SGI?

### Takeaway
The ZX4 is a hollow-body game-improvement iron (MyGolfSpy: "squarely in the game improvement category"). Its forged HT1770 face, 431 stainless body and tungsten in the long and mid irons place it closer to "player's distance" than SGI. Its offset (~3.5–3.7 mm at the 7-iron) and blade length are well short of SGI norms. No MOI figure has been published for it.

### Cited Findings
- **Construction:**
  - Forged HT1770 maraging steel face welded to a 431 stainless steel hollow body. — [Pro Golf Weekly first look](https://progolfweekly.com/first-look-srixons-zx4-irons/)
  - High-density tungsten in the sole of the long and mid irons only, to lower CG and aid launch. — [Pro Golf Weekly](https://progolfweekly.com/first-look-srixons-zx4-irons/); [Golf Guide](https://www.golfguide.com/srixons-zx4-irons/)
  - MainFrame face: thickness patterns milled into the rear of the face, shared with the ZX5 and ZX Utility. Sources describe it inconsistently. — [Golf Guide](https://www.golfguide.com/srixons-zx4-irons/); [Colorado AvidGolfer ZX4](https://coloradoavidgolfer.com/srixon-zx4-a-club-for-all-occasions/)
- **Offset:**
  - Hosel offset 4.4 / 3.7 / 2.8 mm (4-iron / 7-iron / PW) per a Srixon spec table [MFR]. — [Pro Golf Weekly](https://progolfweekly.com/first-look-srixons-zx4-irons/)
  - The official chart gives a 4-iron offset of 4.4 mm vs 3.9 mm for the ZX5. Golf Hub gives the 7-iron as 3.5 mm vs 3.0 mm for the ZX5, which conflicts slightly with 3.7. — [Dunlop Sports ZX4](https://us.dunlopsports.com/srixon/sale/zx4-irons/10313247.html); [Golf Hub specs](https://golfhubz.com/srixon-zx-irons-specs/)
- **Sole width:** 4-iron 23.7 mm vs 21.5 mm for the ZX5. It is the widest sole in the ZX family [MFR]. — [Dunlop Sports ZX4](https://us.dunlopsports.com/srixon/sale/zx4-irons/10313247.html)
- **Blade length:** "barely a millimeter longer than the ZX5". Srixon's engineering director describes a deliberately "better player-focused shorter blade length". — [MyGolfSpy "ZX4: GI, SGI or Player's Distance?"](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/srixon-zx4-irons-gi-sgi-or-players-distance/)
- **Loft:** 7-iron 28.5° (Golf Monthly) or 27° (Golf Hub); sources conflict. — [Golf Monthly ZX4 review](https://www.golfmonthly.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-iron-review); [Golf Hub](https://golfhubz.com/srixon-zx-irons-specs/)
- **Classification:** MyGolfSpy says the sole width suggests SGI, while the topline and offset suggest player's distance, and concludes it is "squarely in the game improvement category". A later MyGolfSpy piece says the ZX4's lofts are "in line with others in the GI category", but with less offset than the Callaway Paradym X it "leans toward the better player side of game-improvement". — [MyGolfSpy ZX4 category](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/srixon-zx4-irons-gi-sgi-or-players-distance/); [MyGolfSpy ZXiR](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/will-the-new-zxir-irons-push-srixon-into-the-big-five/)
- **MOI:** No published figure. Golfstead says hollow construction "significantly increases MOI" vs the solid ZX5, but without numbers [qualitative]. — [Golfstead ZX4 review](https://golfstead.com/srixon-zx4-irons-review)
- **Successors:** The ZX4 Mk II finished 8th for distance in MyGolfSpy testing. The successor-line Srixon ZXiR was MyGolfSpy's 2026 GI forgiveness #1, and the ZXiR HL was third in 2026 SGI forgiveness (9.0). — [MyGolfSpy ZXiR](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/will-the-new-zxir-irons-push-srixon-into-the-big-five/); [MyGolfSpy forgiving 2026](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/best-irons-for-forgiveness-what-the-2026-data-shows/)

### Inferences
- On every metric with data, the ZX4 sits between player's distance and mainstream GI, not SGI:
  - Offset is about 3.5–3.7 mm at the 7-iron. SGI irons generally run more, though I found no SGI offset figures to put a number on that.
  - The blade is compact, close to the ZX5's.
  - The sole is wide for Srixon (23.7 mm at the 4-iron), but its relation to SGI soles is unknown.
- The hollow body plus tungsten probably gives it more MOI than a solid cavity-back of the same size. Its shorter blade, however, likely leaves it well below the ~3,000–3,360 g·cm² of the Ping G425/G710/K15 or the Cleveland Launcher XL. This is an inference with no measured ZX4 MOI behind it.

### Gaps
- No published or measured ZX4 MOI or CG figures.
- No mm-by-mm sole width or offset figures were found for SGI comparators (e.g. G730, Launcher XL Halo) to set beside the ZX4.

## 5. Does more offset, a wider sole or higher MOI actually reduce dispersion in robot or player testing?

### Takeaway
Higher MOI clearly reduces carry and ball-speed loss on heel and toe strikes (Ping's engineer: about 2–3 mph and about 5 yd; robot tests show >90% ball speed kept on extreme toe hits for the best heads). The best robot dispersion in 2026 came from an SGI iron, the Callaway Quantum Max OS. Evidence that offset or sole width on their own reduce dispersion is weak and confounded. Ping's G430 shows lower spin can tighten dispersion even with lower MOI.

### Cited Findings
- **MOI → ball speed retention:** S56 (2,400) vs K15 (3,000) is worth about 2–3 mph and about 5 yd on off-centre strikes [MFR-CLAIM]. — [Golf Digest](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/forgiving-irons)
- **SGI dispersion (robot):** In Golf Digest's 2026 test, the SGI Quantum Max OS had a 95% area of 85.4 sq ft, the tightest of 30 irons and less than half the tightest GI iron. Even the widest SGI head (King Max, 172.7 sq ft) beat 19 of the 30 irons [INDEPENDENT]. — [Golf Digest SGI robot test](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/super-game-improvement-irons-2026-robot-testing-analysis)
- **Distance vs dispersion:** No GI iron in the 2026 robot test led on both distance and dispersion. The most distance-oriented heads tended to disperse more. — [Golf Digest GI robot test](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/game-improvement-irons-2026-robotic-testing-analysis); [Golf Digest distance vs dispersion, 30 irons](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/irons-dispersion-distance-robotic-testing-analysis)
- **Spin vs MOI:** G430 had lower MOI than the G425 but about 23% tighter dispersion, credited to lower spin [MFR-CLAIM]. — [Today's Golfer](https://www.todays-golfer.com/news-and-events/equipment-news/2023/ping-g430-irons-promise-big-distance-gains-over-g425)
- **Heel vs toe asymmetry:** Robot misses on the heel average about 6.5 yd of loss vs 12.3 yd on the toe. The ZipCore XL and G730 kept toe and heel losses to single digits [INDEPENDENT]. — [GOLF.com 2024 toe misses](https://golf.com/gear/irons/2024-irons-toe-misses-robot-testing/)
- **Offset:** About 0.25° of extra face closure for 3.5 mm of offset. Effects are confounded with other design changes. The player-test claim (4 of 5 testers drew it more) is unverified. — [Hireko iron fitting](https://www.hirekogolf.com/modern-guide-to-golf-club-fitting-iron-fitting); [Golf Influence](https://www.golfinfluence.com/skill/what-is-offset-in-golf-irons/)
- **Category-level player data:** GI irons showed the widest average left-right dispersion (7.55 yd) of four categories in Today's Golfer testing. — [Today's Golfer](https://www.todays-golfer.com/news-and-events/equipment-news/golf-iron-categories-comparison-2026/)
- **Category labels and robot data:** Category labels don't predict robot results well. Carry averages were about 155 yd and ball speeds within 0.21 mph across PD, GI and SGI. — [Golf Digest](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/iron-superlatives-categories-robotic-testing-insights)

### Inferences
- The evidence most strongly supports higher heel-toe MOI and low-deep CG (typical of large SGI heads) for keeping ball speed and carry on toe and heel misses, and for tighter dispersion in robot tests (Quantum Max OS). Offset's contribution to accuracy is small (about 0.25°) and mainly helps golfers who leave the face open. Sole width mainly prevents fat shots, which hurts distance consistency more than direction.
- For a high-handicap golfer, a large hollow or undercut SGI head with high published MOI is the evidence-backed choice. Models with the best documented data:
  - Ping G730 (and G425/G710 on used markets)
  - Cleveland Launcher XL / XL Halo / ZipCore XL
  - Callaway Quantum Max OS
  - Cobra King Max
  - Tour Edge Hot Launch Max D

### Gaps
- No isolated robot or player test was found that varies offset only or sole width only.
- Robot testing uses a repeatable swing and does not capture whether offset or sole width helps human golfers with inconsistent face control.
- No vertical-MOI dispersion data exists for irons.
