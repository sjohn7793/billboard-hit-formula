# Srixon ZX4 (2021) irons: forgiveness and accuracy for a long-hitting high handicapper

Research method note: direct page fetching was blocked by the network egress policy in this session (WebFetch failed for golfmonthly.com, pluggedingolf.com and todays-golfer.com, and curl got a 403 from the proxy). Every finding below comes from search-engine extracts and summaries of the cited pages, not from full-page reads. Exact figures should be spot-checked against the live pages before they are quoted as final. Where the search summaries contradicted each other, or looked suspect, this is flagged.

## 1. Specs, construction, category

### Takeaway
The original ZX4 (launched March 2021) is a hollow-body game-improvement (GI) iron with a 28.5° 7-iron, a wide sole, tungsten in the long irons and a milled variable-thickness face. Its category is ambiguous: MyGolfSpy described it as part GI, part SGI and part player's distance, and placed it in its 2021 *super* game-improvement test. Compared with true SGI irons, it has a shorter blade and less offset.

### Cited Findings
- **Launch:** in stores 5 March 2021. Stock steel shaft was the Nippon N.S. Pro 950 Neo (Golf Digest's extract says "950GS Neo"; a GolfWRX user says "950GH Neo", which is the usual name). US price was $1,300 for an 8-piece set. — [Golf Digest launch story](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/srixon-takes-aim-at-the-game-improvement-crowd-with-its-zx4-irons); [GolfWRX forum](https://forums.golfwrx.com/topic/1933608-srixon-zx4-vs-zx4-mk-iidifference-aside-from-cosmetics/)
- **Construction:** AI-assisted design (the "MainFrame" concept). The hollow body has a milled variable-thickness pattern on the back of a high-strength steel face. Tungsten in the base of the 4- to 7-irons lowers the CG for higher launch. Srixon marketed it as the most forgiving ZX model, with a wide sole. — [Golf Digest launch story](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/srixon-takes-aim-at-the-game-improvement-crowd-with-its-zx4-irons)
- Plugged In Golf says the hollow body widens the performance zone and the milled face pattern keeps ball speed up across it. — [Plugged In Golf](https://pluggedingolf.com/srixon-zx4-irons-review/)
- **7-iron spec (Dunlop/Srixon US spec sheet):** 28.5° loft, 62.0° lie, 3.7 mm offset, 37" length, D2 swing weight in steel. — [Dunlop Sports / Srixon ZX4 page](https://us.dunlopsports.com/srixon/sale/zx4-irons/30204037.html)
- **Versus the ZX5:** the ZX4 7-iron is 28.5° against 31° for the ZX5. The ZX4 has a thicker topline, a wider sole and more offset, which Golf Monthly says "slicers will appreciate". — [Golf Monthly ZX4 review](https://www.golfmonthly.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-iron-review)
- **Category, per MyGolfSpy at launch:** "super-forgiving hollow-bodied game improvement player's distance iron with forged feel". The sole width suggests SGI, while the topline and offset suggest player's distance. The blade is shorter than Cleveland's GI/SGI irons. — [MyGolfSpy: "Srixon ZX4 Irons: GI, SGI or Player's Distance?"](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/srixon-zx4-irons-gi-sgi-or-players-distance/)
- MyGolfSpy's 2021 Most Wanted test put the original ZX4 in the **super game-improvement** category and the ZX5 in game-improvement. — [MyGolfSpy 2021 Most Wanted SGI](https://mygolfspy.com/buyers-guides/irons/2021-most-wanted-super-game-improvement-iron/) (stated in later MyGolfSpy Mk II coverage: [MyGolfSpy ZX4 Mk II review](https://mygolfspy.com/reviews/game-improvement-irons/srixon-zx4-mk-ii-irons-review/))
- Golf Monthly calls the ZX4 a "hybrid-style iron with a chunky sole and lots of offset" (said in contrast to the slimmer Mk II). — [Golf Monthly ZX4 Mk II review](https://www.golfmonthly.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-mk-ii-iron-review)

### Inferences
- A 28.5° 7-iron is standard for a modern GI iron, not extreme. For a golfer who already hits far, that strength mainly adds gapping problems at the top of the bag (4- to 6-iron) rather than helping.
- 3.7 mm of offset in the 7-iron is moderate. Dedicated SGI irons usually carry more, which suggests the ZX4 gives a slicer less anti-slice help than an SGI model would.
- In practice the ZX4 is a "max-GI" iron: more forgiving than the ZX5, but more compact and less offset than SGI benchmarks.

### Gaps
- No full loft table for the original ZX4 (4-iron to AW) was retrievable. Only the 7-iron spec was confirmed.
- I could not confirm the face material ("SUP10" was named in the brief) or whether Srixon applied the "MainFrame" branding to the original ZX4 rather than only to later models. Golf Digest's extract says only "high-strength steel face".
- Sole width in mm was not found.
- Stock graphite shaft and UK/EU stock shaft options were not confirmed.
- No official launch window was found. Golf Digest and Golf Monthly describe it qualitatively as "high launching".

## 2. Independent test results (forgiveness, dispersion, mishit ball speed)

### Takeaway
UK and US independent reviewers rate the ZX4 as very forgiving for its size, with consistent carry on off-centre hits and tight dispersion. Golf Monthly gave it 4.5 stars, and Plugged In Golf found thin shots lost only 10-15 yards. No source with retrievable numbers showed it beating dedicated SGI irons on forgiveness. MyGolfSpy's ZX4 ranking in its 2021 SGI test could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- **Golf Monthly (June 2021, 4.5/5, FlightScope X3):**
  - The 28.5° 7-iron "regularly just exceeded 180 yards" of carry and peaked at 184 yards. Spin stayed below 5,000 rpm.
  - Dispersion was tight and carry consistent. Golf Monthly noted consistent distance on off-centre strikes.
  - Pros: speed off the face, forgiveness with high launch. Con: expensive for a 6-piece set.
  - Headline: "A Perfect Option For The Higher Handicapper?" The verdict: "certainly worth considering" for golfers who want distance and forgiveness with a traditional look.
  - — [Golf Monthly ZX4 review](https://www.golfmonthly.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-iron-review)
- **Plugged In Golf:** forgiving. Thin shots still carried far enough to lose only about 10-15 yards. The hollow body and milled face keep ball speed up across the face. — [Plugged In Golf ZX4 review](https://pluggedingolf.com/srixon-zx4-irons-review/)
- **TGW "The Golf Guide" (retailer editorial):**
  - Measured averages (club not named, apparently a 7-iron): 112.5 mph ball speed, 18.9° launch, 4,037 rpm spin, 166 yd carry.
  - Headline: "Hollow Design For High Launch, Highly Forgiving Performance".
  - One search summary attributed this review to Today's Golfer, but the URL is tgw.com (The Golf Warehouse), so the attribution is doubtful.
  - — [TGW Golf Guide ZX4 review](https://www.tgw.com/golf-guide/srixon-zx4-irons-review/)
- **Today's Golfer** has a ZX4 review page titled "Fast, forged, and forgiving in 2024? Srixon ZX4 iron Review", but its test data could not be retrieved. — [Today's Golfer ZX4](https://todays-golfer.com/equipment/golf-clubs/irons/srixon/zx/srixon-zx4-irons)
- **Golf.com ClubTest 2021:** a hollow-body iron that adds forgiveness while keeping "the feel and versatility of a forged blade". Testers praised its consistent feel and sound. No ranking was visible in the extract. — [Golf.com ClubTest 2021](https://golf.com/gear/irons/srixon-zx4-irons-clubtest-2021/)
- **Golf Digest Hot List:**
  - A 2021 Hot List page exists in the game-improvement category. Golf Digest's testers called the ZX4 easy to hit and high-launching, especially for higher handicappers. — [Golf Digest Hot List 2021: Srixon ZX4](https://www.golfdigest.com/hot-list-2021/golf-clubs/game-improvement-irons/srixon-zx4)
  - Golf Galaxy and Golfstead describe the ZX4 as a **2022** Golf Digest Hot List **Gold** winner. — [Golf Galaxy listing](https://www.golfgalaxy.com/p/srixon-zx4-irons-21srxmzx4stl5wxxxirn/21srxmzx4stl5wxxxirn); [Golfstead](https://golfstead.com/srixon-zx4-irons-review)
  - Medal status for 2021 is unconfirmed, and the 2022 claim comes from retailer/affiliate sources.
- **Golfstead (affiliate review site):**
  - Scored distance 9.3/10 and forgiveness 9.9/10.
  - Spin is "significantly lower than the ZX5", which means more runout and less stopping power into greens.
  - Argued there isn't enough gain in distance and forgiveness over the ZX5 to justify the thicker profile.
  - — [Golfstead ZX4 review](https://golfstead.com/srixon-zx4-irons-review)
- **Driving Range Heroes (July 2021):** "designed to go long and straight". — [Driving Range Heroes](https://drivingrangeheroes.com/srixon-zx4-irons-review/)
- **MyGolfSpy 2021 Most Wanted SGI test:**
  - The ZX4 was in the field and is mentioned in field notes about its sole design. Its strokes-gained and forgiveness ranks did not appear in any retrievable extract.
  - Winner: Cobra F-Max Airspeed. Its ranks appear inconsistently across extracts: one says 1st in strokes gained, 2nd in forgiveness and 9th in distance; another says 2nd in strokes gained and 6th in forgiveness.
  - Honma Beres 2-Star: 3rd in strokes gained, 4th in forgiveness.
  - The ZX4 was not named among the top finishers.
  - — [MyGolfSpy 2021 Most Wanted SGI](https://mygolfspy.com/buyers-guides/irons/2021-most-wanted-super-game-improvement-iron/)
- **National Club Golfer:** no review of the original 2021 ZX4 was found. Their 2023 Mk II review is covered in section 5.
- **Rick Shiels / TXG:** no specific ZX4 (2021) test data was found within this session's search budget.

### Inferences
- Across sources, the ZX4's forgiveness strength is **distance consistency on mishits**. Its hollow-body face keeps carry loss small: 10-15 yards on thins per Plugged In, and consistent carry off-centre per Golf Monthly.
- Low spin plus strong lofts suit a high-swing-speed player for distance, but can reduce stopping power on greens. Golfstead raised this, and Golf Monthly measured spin below 5,000 rpm on a 28.5° 7-iron.
- The ZX4 did not obviously win MyGolfSpy's 2021 SGI category, which went to the Cobra F-Max Airspeed. This is weak evidence that the ZX4 is not at the top of the SGI class on forgiveness, though its actual rank is unknown.

### Gaps
- The ZX4's MyGolfSpy 2021 SGI ranks (strokes gained, forgiveness, accuracy, distance) and its Golf Digest 2021 scores (performance, forgiveness and playability out of 10) were not retrievable.
- No dispersion figures in yards, and no ball-speed drop on heel/toe hits in mph, were found for the original ZX4.
- Golfalot, Rick Shiels and TXG data for the original ZX4 was not found.

## 3. Known weaknesses for high handicappers

### Takeaway
The main documented weaknesses for high handicappers are:
- strong lofts and low spin, so less stopping power, a lower peak height than SGI irons, and gapping issues at the top of the set;
- a compact, less offset look than SGI irons, which gives less anti-slice help;
- off-centre feedback that some reviewers found harsher than expected for a GI iron (said of the Mk II).

### Cited Findings
- Spin is "significantly lower than the ZX5", with more runout and less stopping power on greens. — [Golfstead](https://golfstead.com/srixon-zx4-irons-review)
- Golf Monthly measured spin below 5,000 rpm on the 7-iron. — [Golf Monthly ZX4 review](https://www.golfmonthly.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-iron-review)
- NCG found the near-identical-loft Mk II's "super strong lofts" produced a "pretty low flight" for their tester. — [NCG ZX4 Mk II review](https://www.nationalclubgolfer.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-mkii-irons-review/)
- Golf Monthly's con for the Mk II: it "may not inspire confidence in the hands of higher handicappers". — [Golf Monthly ZX4 Mk II review](https://www.golfmonthly.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-mk-ii-iron-review)
- GolfMagic's con for the Mk II: off-centre hits give "more unwanted feedback than you'd expect from a game-improvement iron". GolfMagic positions it as a mid-handicap set. — [GolfMagic ZX4 Mk II review](https://www.golfmagic.com/equipment/irons/srixon-zx4-mkii-irons-review-perfect-set-mid-handicap-golfer)
- MyGolfSpy says the ZX4 has less offset and a shorter blade than typical SGI irons, which are player-leaning traits. — [MyGolfSpy](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/srixon-zx4-irons-gi-sgi-or-players-distance/)
- A later MyGolfSpy piece says the ZX4-family lofts (28.5° 7-iron) are "in line with others in the GI category", with more than 25% less offset than the Callaway Paradym X. — [MyGolfSpy (Srixon ZX4 coverage)](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/srixon-zx4-irons-gi-sgi-or-players-distance/) (attribution from a search extract; it may come from a different MyGolfSpy article)
- Golf Monthly's main con for the original: price, since the set is only 6 pieces. — [Golf Monthly ZX4 review](https://www.golfmonthly.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-iron-review)

### Inferences
- For a high handicapper who already hits far, the ZX4's distance-oriented, low-spin design does not add value they need. The main risk is long-iron shots that come in flat and run through greens. A dedicated SGI or higher-launching GI iron (weaker lofts, more offset, higher MOI) is more likely to cut dispersion and improve green-holding.
- The user's 7-iron carry is not given. As a reference point, Golf Monthly's tester carried the ZX4 7-iron 180+ yards. A long hitter could end up with large gaps between PW and wedges, and overly long 7- and 8-iron carries.

### Gaps
- No quantified dispersion data from users (shot-pattern widths) was found. Forum evidence is anecdotal.
- Gapping data across the full set was not retrieved.

## 4. Comparison vs 2021 peers on forgiveness

### Takeaway
No source with retrievable data tested the ZX4 head-to-head against its 2021 peers. MyGolfSpy ran the peers in its separate 2021 GI test: Mizuno JPX 921 Hot Metal won, Ping G425 was runner-up and Srixon's own ZX5 placed 4th. The ZX4 sat in the SGI test, which Cobra's F-Max Airspeed won. Category forgiveness therefore has to be inferred.

### Cited Findings
- **MyGolfSpy 2021 Most Wanted GI irons:**
  - Mizuno JPX 921 Hot Metal won: 1st in strokes gained, **9th in forgiveness**. Testers found it bulky.
  - Ping G425 was 2nd: 2nd in strokes gained, **14th in forgiveness**.
  - Srixon ZX5 ranked 4th in strokes gained and **12th in forgiveness**. A forum post on the test thread says the ZX5 still beat the G425 on forgiveness.
  - — [MyGolfSpy 2021 Most Wanted GI](https://mygolfspy.com/buyers-guides/irons/2021-most-wanted-game-improvement-iron/); [MyGolfSpy forum thread](https://forum.mygolfspy.com/topic/45010-2021-most-wanted-game-improvement-irons/)
- **MyGolfSpy 2021 SGI category:** won by the Cobra F-Max Airspeed. The ZX4 was included. — [MyGolfSpy 2021 Most Wanted SGI](https://mygolfspy.com/buyers-guides/irons/2021-most-wanted-super-game-improvement-iron/)
- **Lofts:** one comparison site lists the JPX 921 Hot Metal 7-iron at 29° and the Ping G425 at 30°, against 28.5° for the ZX4 per Srixon's spec sheet. — [Hole The Putt comparison](https://holetheputt.com/mizuno-jpx-921-hot-metal-vs-ping-g425-irons-comparison/); [Dunlop Sports](https://us.dunlopsports.com/srixon/sale/zx4-irons/30204037.html)
- **Golf Insider UK, on the later ZXi4:** it places the set below the largest game-improvement irons for forgiveness, with under 5 mph ball-speed drop-off on toe/heel strikes. This concerns the successor, but the relative positioning is likely similar. — [Golf Insider UK ZXi4 review](https://golfinsideruk.com/srixon-zxi-4-irons-review/)
- A GolfWRX thread compares the ZX4 and Ping G425 directly. It is anecdotal user opinion only and was not retrieved in detail. — [GolfWRX: Srixon ZX4 vs Ping G425](https://forums.golfwrx.com/topic/1952876-srixon-zx-4-vrs-ping-425-irons/)

### Inferences
- Because MyGolfSpy grouped it with SGI irons, the ZX4 was judged forgiving enough for that class. It did not win the class, and the Cobra F-Max Airspeed took the title.
- Against the GI peers named in the brief (G425, JPX 921 HM, Radspeed, SIM2 Max, Mavrik, T300), the ZX4 is probably similar to or slightly more forgiving than its own ZX5, which beat the G425 on MyGolfSpy forgiveness. It is likely less forgiving than true SGI models such as Ping G425/G430 **Max**/HL, Callaway Mavrik Max / Rogue ST Max OS, TaylorMade SIM2 Max OS and Cobra F-Max. This is a reasoned inference, not a measured result.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head data was found between the ZX4 and the Callaway Mavrik / Rogue ST Max, TaylorMade SIM2 Max, Cobra Radspeed / LTDx or Titleist T300.
- 7-iron lofts for the Mavrik, SIM2 Max, Radspeed and T300 could not be verified. Search returned only spam pages, so manufacturer spec sheets are needed.
  - Unverified from background knowledge, not to be cited: Mavrik standard 7-iron about 27°, SIM2 Max about 28.5°, Radspeed about 27.5-28°, T300 (2021) about 29°.
- No ZX4 ranking in the MyGolfSpy SGI table was retrievable.

## 5. Successors (ZX4 Mk II 2023, ZXi4 2025) and whether upgrading helps forgiveness

### Takeaway
Neither successor is a clear forgiveness upgrade. The Mk II made the head more compact (slimmer sole, thinner topline, less offset) with the 7-iron loft unchanged at 28.5°. The ZXi4 refined looks, sound and feel, with mixed reports on offset and launch. Reviewers broadly judge mishit forgiveness as similar across all three generations.

### Cited Findings
- **ZX4 Mk II changes:**
  - More compact head, less offset and a thinner topline than the "hybrid-style" original. Golf Monthly still calls it the largest and most forgiving iron in the Mk II range. — [Golf Monthly ZX4 Mk II](https://www.golfmonthly.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-mk-ii-iron-review)
  - Spec listings show a 7-iron at 28.5°, 62° lie, 3.4 mm offset and C9 swing weight, against 3.7 mm and D2 for the original. — [Golf House listing](https://golfhouse.com/srixon-zx4-graphite-regular-91484.html); [Dunlop Sports (original)](https://us.dunlopsports.com/srixon/sale/zx4-irons/30204037.html)
  - Loft Chart says the Mk II carries over the ZX4 lofts unchanged. Plugged In Golf describes stronger lofts. The sources conflict. — [Loft Chart](https://loftchart.com/srixon/zx4-mkii-irons/); [Plugged In Golf Mk II](https://pluggedingolf.com/srixon-zx4-mk-ii-irons-review/)
  - Golfstead says the Mk II has a slimmer sole and longer blade, and forgiveness is "just as good". — [Golfstead Mk II](https://golfstead.com/srixon-zx4-mk-ii-irons-review)
  - Plugged In Golf says the Mk II has a quieter "snap" at impact than the original and is "not dramatically more forgiving than the ZX5 Mk II". — [Plugged In Golf Mk II](https://pluggedingolf.com/srixon-zx4-mk-ii-irons-review/)
  - The stock steel shaft changed from the Nippon 950GH Neo to the KBS Tour Lite. One GolfWRX user found the Mk II launched lower (anecdotal). — [GolfWRX](https://forums.golfwrx.com/topic/1933608-srixon-zx4-vs-zx4-mk-iidifference-aside-from-cosmetics/)
- **MyGolfSpy 2023 (Mk II):**
  - 4th overall among GI irons, **4th for forgiveness**, **2nd for accuracy**, 8th for distance. — [MyGolfSpy ZX4 Mk II review](https://mygolfspy.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-mk-ii-irons-review/)
  - Another MyGolfSpy page calls it the 3rd-best GI iron, an all-rounder rather than a category leader. — [MyGolfSpy ZX4 Mk II](https://mygolfspy.com/reviews/game-improvement-irons/srixon-zx4-mk-ii/)
  - Note: MyGolfSpy tested the Mk II as **GI**, whereas the original sat in **SGI** in 2021.
- **Other Mk II tests:**
  - Golf Monthly (Foresight GCQuad): 7-iron average carry 178 yd, peak height 36 yd, spin about 5,000 rpm. — [Golf Monthly Mk II](https://www.golfmonthly.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-mk-ii-iron-review)
  - GolfMagic reported "142 mph ball speed… just over 5000 RPM… 210 yards" with a 7-iron on better strikes. That is an implausible 7-iron ball speed for an amateur review and is possibly a transcription error, so treat it as suspect. — [GolfMagic Mk II](https://www.golfmagic.com/equipment/irons/srixon-zx4-mkii-irons-review-perfect-set-mid-handicap-golfer)
  - NCG: "soft and forgiving on off-centre strikes", but the strong lofts gave the tester low flight. — [NCG Mk II](https://www.nationalclubgolfer.com/reviews/irons/srixon-zx4-mkii-irons-review/)
- **ZXi4 (2025) changes and verdicts:**
  - 7-iron at 28.5°, 62° lie, 3.2 mm offset, 37.25". — [Fairway Golf USA listing](https://www.fairwaygolfusa.com/category/select/pid/813541)
  - Golfstead: offset "substantially less" than the Mk II. Launch is 1-1.5° lower with slightly more spin. Ball-speed and directional forgiveness are not significantly different from the predecessor. Feedback is clearer. Distance is average or below for 2025. — [Golfstead ZXi4](https://golfstead.com/srixon-zxi4-irons-review)
  - Independent Golf Reviews: hollow body with MainFrame milled channels, the most offset in the ZXi line, and forgiving. — [Independent Golf Reviews](https://www.independentgolfreviews.com/srixon-zxi4-irons-review/)
  - Plugged In Golf: more ball speed and forgiveness than the ZX4 Mk II, strong lofts, low spin, a "don't fix what isn't broken" approach. — [Plugged In Golf ZXi4](https://pluggedingolf.com/srixon-zxi4-irons-review/)
  - Golf Digest Hot List (Gold, per search extract): tight dispersion on mis-hits and penetrating mid-high flight. Some testers found the sound and feel firm, accuracy slightly inconsistent, and the longer irons lost height and distance on slight mis-hits. — [Golf Digest ZXi4](https://www.golfdigest.com/hot-list/irons/srixon-zxi4)
  - Golf Insider UK: under 5 mph ball-speed drop on toe/heel strikes, but below the largest GI irons for forgiveness. — [Golf Insider UK](https://golfinsideruk.com/srixon-zxi-4-irons-review/)
- Srixon's 2025 range also includes the **ZXiR**, with a 30° 7-iron per Golf Insider UK. This is a separate, more SGI/launch-oriented model rather than a ZX4 successor. — [Golf Insider UK best GI irons](https://golfinsideruk.com/best-game-improvement-irons/); [MyGolfSpy on ZXiR](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/will-the-new-zxir-irons-push-srixon-into-the-big-five/)

### Inferences
- Upgrading from the ZX4 to the Mk II or ZXi4 is unlikely to improve forgiveness meaningfully. Both successors trend more compact, with less offset and, for the ZXi4, lower launch, which works against a high handicapper's needs. The Mk II's strong MyGolfSpy accuracy rank (2nd among GI irons) is the best evidence for a dispersion benefit, and it is a single test.
- Within Srixon, the more relevant step for a high handicapper wanting more forgiveness and higher launch is probably the ZXiR (weaker 30° 7-iron), not a newer ZX4 generation. Its forgiveness should be checked in its own reviews.

### Gaps
- No controlled ZX4 vs Mk II vs ZXi4 mishit data from the same test was found.
- ZXi4 offset reports conflict: Golfstead says less, Independent Golf Reviews says the most in the ZXi line. The two statements can both be true because they compare against different models.
